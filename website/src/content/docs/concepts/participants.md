title: Participants & roles
description: Each agenda has its own roster of people who can be assigned to items and recorded as speakers.

Every agenda carries its own **participant roster** — the people taking part in that meeting. Assign them to agenda items ("General Evaluator report — Jane"), and record them as speakers during [sub-item rounds](/concepts/sub-item-loops/).

Rosters are **per agenda**: people are not shared between agendas, and there is no global address book. A weekly meeting typically starts by duplicating last week's structure via [CSV export/import](/planning/csv-import/), which carries the people along.

## How people get onto the roster

1. **The participants page** — from the agenda editor, follow the **participants** link and add names directly. Names sort alphabetically.
2. **Assigning items** — the add-item row and the item edit dialog both have an assignee dropdown listing the roster.
3. **CSV import** — any name in the `person` column of an [imported CSV](/planning/csv-import/) is created as a participant automatically and assigned to that row's item. The same name used on several rows becomes one person.

## Assigning items

- Pick an assignee in the item's edit dialog, or
- click the **"Unassigned"** link on an item card for a quick one-tap assign dialog.

Assignees show on the item card, on the display screen's schedule, and in the planned-schedule row for each item.

## Recording who actually spoke

During a sub-item round, the [control screen](/running/control-screen/) shows a participant dropdown. Whatever you select is saved on that specific round — so the [report](/running/reports/) can say "Speaker 2 — Jane Doe, 6:12" even if the item itself was assigned to nobody.

## Renaming and deleting

- **Rename** via the pencil icon on the participants page.
- **Delete** after a native confirmation. Deleting a person only **unassigns** them: their items remain (now unassigned), and past meeting reports keep showing the name as it was recorded at the time. Nothing in history is rewritten.
